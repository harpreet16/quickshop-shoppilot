## Executive conclusion

QuickShop and the new B2 share the highest execution completion (20/20 each). QuickShop produced 48 recommendations across 17 queries, and abstained on three. However, only 25 of its 41 top-three recommendation slots have a matching recorded page result with structured natural-fiber share >=50%. It also made unsupported product/material claims after search or extraction failures. This run supports a reliability/traceability advantage, not a proven shopping-accuracy or seller-safety win.

B2 now completes 20/20 queries and returns 57 recommendation/sourcing entries across 20 answers. It averages 6.21 seconds, but only four entries link to individual product-like URLs: 47 link to homepages and six to category/search pages. B0 completes 15 queries using a fixed jeans catalog; B1 completes 14 and all 42 top-three entries lack prices. These limitations still prevent a fair overall shopping-quality ranking.

## Findings that change the interpretation

### 1. Scoring succeeds on unsupported inputs

- **d2q3:** five oxford recommendations claim 100% cotton even though no returned page record has a structured fiber share. Four records are unavailable, one partial, and three additional reads were denied.
- **d2q5:** search-rate-limit fallback recommends generic-looking Target/Gap/ASOS URLs and invented-looking product facts. Only the fifth recommendation has matching structured fiber evidence; none of the first three does.
- **d2q6:** four natural-fiber shirts are recommended without matching structured fiber shares. The single record with a known natural-fiber share describes an over-budget cotton/wool item that was not recommended.
- **d2q2:** Carhartt has variant-dependent composition and Gap's structured share is 0, but the scoring inputs turn both into unqualified 100% cotton. This is an evidence-to-scoring override, not independent verification.

### 2. Product discovery and extraction still admit category pages

- d2q14 recommends Walmart's `/c/kp/mens-slim-fit-henleys` category URL as a specific $16.95 shirt.
- d2q15 recommends a Levi's category/filter URL as a particular $42 button-down.
- d2q20 recommends a Macy's dress-pants category URL as one particular cotton product and supplies 100% cotton to scoring despite unknown structured fiber share.
- B1 frequently recommends category/editorial pages, including Wirecutter, Byrdie and GQ, rather than individual purchasable offers. All 42 successful top-three entries print `($None)` while still receiving numerical price scores.

### 3. Abstention labels are not unquestionable ground truth

- QuickShop abstains on d2q24 and d2q25: 2/3 of the designated abstention cases.
- It recommends two items on d2q23; both recorded page results state 100% cashmere and USD prices below $50. That disagrees with the benchmark's expected abstention, but does not alone prove either a model error or authentic products. The Walmart link is a different apparent size segment and the Target link specifies 2X despite the recorded clarification answer L, so fit was not established.
- On d2q19, QuickShop abstains after requiring natural fibers for a -15°C waterproof jacket. The user's query does not require natural fibers, and `eval/dataset2.py` explicitly allows practical synthetic insulation. This is an over-restrictive response, not an automatic safety success.

### 4. Seller and task-specific claims are weaker than they look

- Known-brand/domain heuristics are narrated as verified domain ages and external-review counts. These are not independently measured trust labels.
- Established sellers such as Lands' End and L.L.Bean are rejected in some outputs for missing recognition/review signals. Missing data is not evidence that a seller is dangerous.
- d2q17 does not establish ethical sourcing; recognizing a retailer and confirming wool content does not answer the ethics requirement.
- d2q7 and d2q17 convert EUR page prices into USD without a recorded exchange-rate source.
- Some answers use 'safe' or 'safe to buy' despite the intended risk-based wording.

### 5. Failures and overhead need better capture

- Historically, B2 completed only 5/20 queries in the earlier file; the new file completes all 20. Do not retain the old 15 B2 failures in current totals or cherry-pick its old successful answers. B0/B1 still fail on the first five queries; B1 additionally fails d2q19.
- Failed subprocess results often lack stderr/exit diagnostics, and cached output files can survive failures. Do not analyze those output strings as fresh successes.
- The five-page budget was enforced: 100 actual returned records plus 22 denied attempts, not 122 successful page reads.
- 24 scoring validation errors, largely explicit nulls passed to non-nullable parameters, add avoidable retries.
- The slowest response was d2q23: 887.49 s (14.79 min). The priciest by recorded Firecrawl credits was d2q25: 107 credits, ending in abstention.

## Why the stock metrics table is not used verbatim

`eval/metrics.py` needs correction before it can be treated as a trustworthy evaluation summary:

1. QuickShop picks are identified by price occurrence, iterated in tool-call order and deduplicated by price. d2q1 has two different $24.99 products, so this loses a recommendation and does not preserve the actual top-three ranking.
2. The recommendation-section splitter only recognizes 'not recommended'; d2q24 uses different rejection wording, allowing a rejected $36.75 item to be miscounted as recommended.
3. B0 parsing assumes a 'Recommended products' heading. d2q18 has three real recommendations under numbered headings and is missed by that parser.
4. B1/B2 prices are frequently unknown, approximate or ranges. Treating missing values as either budget compliance or failure gives misleading rates; they need explicit unknown coverage.
5. Baseline Firecrawl credits are hard-coded to zero, but this artifact contains no such measurements. Unknown cost is not zero cost.
6. Page-read counting includes budget-denied calls; successful evidence returns must be separated from attempts.

## Recommended next steps

1. Preserve this run as historical evidence; rerun after the provider migration with all four systems configured consistently.
2. Capture exit code, stderr, provider/model identifiers, source URLs, currencies and per-system cost/usage; exclude stale output on failed executions.
3. Evaluate recommendations by stable product ID/URL and final rank, not price-string matches. Keep unknown values explicit.
4. Require evidence-backed composition/price inputs before scoring; reject category/editorial URLs as specific product offers.
5. Separate execution completion, usable answer rate, evidence support, task fit, abstention behavior and independently labeled seller risk. Review low-price 'abstain' labels against actual offers rather than assuming impossibility.

Limitations: this is a mixed-source, single-observation-per-system/query comparison. Live availability, authenticity, fit, materials, exchange rates and seller safety were not independently verified. The new B2 file does not record provider/model identity, grounding calls, or token/search cost; grounding cannot be established from its prose alone. Original run JSON and application/evaluation code were not changed.
